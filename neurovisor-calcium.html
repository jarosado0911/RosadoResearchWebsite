Neuro-VISOR · Calcium dynamics

Calcium in VR, down to the soma.

The calcium model of our SIAM paper, running live in Neuro-VISOR on reconstructed neurons. Click and hold on a dendrite to let calcium in. If enough comes in, ryanodine receptors on the ER answer with more, and a wave of calcium-induced calcium release runs to the cell body. The soma is no longer one node: it is a 3D lattice of cells with the ER inside, so you can watch the wave arrive at its surface and work its way in.

01 · In VR

A held click becomes a wave.

Recorded in Neuro-VISOR (the calcium-model branch) on a reconstructed CA1 pyramidal cell. The click is held for 20 ms on the thick apical trunk, 35 µm from the soma. Left: the soma close up, its surface colored by cytosolic calcium and the ER visible inside. Right: the whole cell in the VR room. One frame is 2 ms of simulated time.

  1. At rest

    Cytosol at 50 nM, the ER full at 250 µM. Every pump and leak balances, so nothing changes until you touch the cell.

  2. The click

    Holding the click lets a steady influx of calcium in through the membrane at the touched nodes, for as long as it is held.

  3. The ER answers

    The local calcium crosses the ryanodine receptors' threshold. They open, the ER releases, and the released calcium opens the next receptors.

  4. Into the soma

    The wave reaches the soma's surface at about 70 ms. In the lattice it spreads in from there, and the ER empties from its surface inward.

  5. The cell fills

    The wave spreads on through the dendrites while the soma keeps filling: about 54 µM on average by 400 ms. The pumps bring it back down over seconds, far longer than this 300 ms clip.

02 · The model

Two compartments in every dendrite.

Each node of the neuron's 1D graph is a cylinder of radius R with the ER a coaxial cylinder of radius r=\rho R inside it, \rho=0.625. The cytosol is the annulus between them. Calcium diffuses along both and binds to a buffer in each; the membranes exchange it through fluxes J [mol m⁻² s⁻¹].

Cytosol and ER

\begin{aligned}\partial_t c_c&=\frac{1}{A_c}\partial_x\big(A_cD_c\,\partial_xc_c\big)+\kappa_b^-(b_{\mathrm{tot}}-b)-\kappa_b^+b\,c_c+g_{PM}J_{PM}+g_{ERc}J_{ERM}\\ \partial_t c_e&=\frac{1}{A_e}\partial_x\big(A_eD_e\,\partial_xc_e\big)+\kappa_{be}^-(b_{e,\mathrm{tot}}-b_e)-\kappa_{be}^+b_e\,c_e-g_{ERe}J_{ERM}+g_{ERe}J_{SOC}\end{aligned}

The buffers calbindin b and calreticulin b_e diffuse and react the same way. A_c=\pi(R^2-r^2) and A_e=\pi r^2 are the cross sections, so a thickening dendrite carries more calcium past a point.

Area to volume

g_{PM}=\frac{2R}{R^2-r^2},\quad g_{ERc}=\frac{2r}{R^2-r^2},\quad g_{ERe}=\frac{2}{r}

The factors turn a flux through a membrane into a rate of change of concentration. Thin branches have far more membrane per volume, so the same flux moves their calcium much faster.

Plasma membrane

\begin{gathered}J_{PM}=-\rho_PI_P\frac{c_c^2}{K_P^2+c_c^2}-s_N\rho_NI_N\frac{c_c}{K_N+c_c}\\+v_{l,p}(c_o-c_c)+J_{\mathrm{click}}\end{gathered}

PMCA and NCX pump calcium out (the VR app uses five times the paper's NCX, s_N=5), a leak lets it in, and the click adds an influx while it is held.

ER membrane

\begin{gathered}J_{ERM}=\rho_R(o_1+o_2)I_R\frac{c_e-c_c}{c_{e,\mathrm{ref}}}\\-\rho_SI_S\frac{c_c}{(K_S+c_c)\,c_e}+v_{l,e}(c_e-c_c)\end{gathered}

Ryanodine receptors release, SERCA pumps back, a leak balances them. The leak velocities v_{l,e},v_{l,p} are not free: they are set so the resting state has zero net flux.

Ryanodine receptor

\begin{gathered}\dot o_2=k_b^+c_c^3o_1-k_b^-o_2,\quad \dot c_1=k_a^-o_1-k_a^+c_c^4c_1,\\ \dot c_2=k_c^+o_1-k_c^-c_2,\quad o_1=1-c_1-o_2-c_2\end{gathered}

The steep c_c^3 and c_c^4 make release all-or-nothing: below a threshold the receptors stay shut, above it they open and recruit their neighbours.

Store-operated refill

J_{SOC}=\rho_{SOC}\,f(c_e)\,\frac{I_{SOC}\ln(10\,c_o/c_e)}{F\,z\,A_{\mathrm{Orai}}}

When the ER runs low, Orai channels open (f grows with the depletion) and refill it from outside the cell.

03 · Volume diffusion

The soma, resolved in 3D.

On the cable the soma used to be a single node, so it filled all at once, as if it were perfectly mixed. Now it is a lattice of cubic cells, and calcium has to diffuse through it.

  1. Cells

    Cubes of side 1.5 µm fill the soma sphere: 1,357 of them for the CA1 cell's 10.2 µm soma. The ER is a concentric sphere of radius \rho R.

  2. Volumes and areas

    Each cell's share of cytosol and ER is sampled, and so is its share of both membranes. A cell cut by the ER sphere gets ER membrane and both kinds of fluxes.

  3. Coupling

    Neighbouring cells exchange calcium through their shared faces, by the same finite-volume diffusion as the cable, in both compartments.

  4. Junctions

    Where a dendrite leaves the soma, its last node is joined to the surface cell it meets, conserving mass. The old 1D nodes inside the soma are taken out.

  5. Coloring

    The soma's surface and ER mesh are colored by trilinear interpolation over the cells of their own phase, so cubes don't show as a checkerboard.

04 · Numerics

Four factorizations, then only solves.

Diffusion is linear and stiff and is taken implicitly; every reaction and membrane flux is taken explicitly. Each species has its own diffusion matrix, factored once when the cell loads.

SBDF2

\frac{3u^{k+1}-4u^k+u^{k-1}}{2\Delta t}=\mathcal D\,u^{k+1}+2F(u^k)-F(u^{k-1})

u=(c_c,c_e,b,b_e) on every node and lattice cell. The four matrices I-\tfrac23\Delta t\,\mathcal D are LU-factored once (CSparse). The RyR states are advanced by backward Euler, solved in closed form at every node. With \Delta t=50 µs and 10 steps per frame, one simulated second of the CA1 cell takes about 40 seconds of real time.

Refinement

Before the solver starts, the cell's graph is refined until no edge is longer than 1 µm, so a wave front spans several nodes. A minimum radius keeps the thinnest branches' area-to-volume factors from making the explicit fluxes unstable.
