Inside SpineBuilder.

A calcium simulation is only as good as the shape it runs on. SpineBuilder is a desktop application I wrote that turns dendritic spines, ideal or reconstructed from electron microscopy, and whole neurons into closed, labeled meshes ready for finite element simulation in ug4. It has three tabs, one for each way of getting a mesh.

Tab 01 · Ideal Spine Builder

A spine from a dozen numbers.

    The math

    The shape of the head

    The spine is a stack of polygonal rings. The neck keeps its radius; the head widens over its first third, stays wide, and closes over its last third:

    r(y)=\begin{cases} r_n, & y\le y_h \\ r_n+(r_h-r_n)\,\dfrac{3(y-y_h)}{\ell_h}, & y_h<y\le y_h+\tfrac{\ell_h}{3}\\ r_h, & y\le y_h+\tfrac{2\ell_h}{3}\\ r_n+(r_h-r_n)\,\dfrac{3(y_h+\ell_h-y)}{\ell_h}, & y\le y_h+\ell_h \end{cases}

    with y_h = R + \ell_n: the dendrite radius plus the neck length. The flat cap at the tip is the synapse.

    When the mesh is valid

    The dendrite is a regular n-gon of radius R with a vertex on top. The neck opening has to fit on the two faces next to that vertex, the ER inside the polygon's inscribed circle, and the spine apparatus opening on the ER:

    r_n < R\sin\frac{2\pi}{n},\qquad r_{er} < R\cos\frac{\pi}{n},\qquad r_{sa} < r_{er}\sin\frac{2\pi}{n_{er}}

    The spine apparatus must also be narrower than the spine at every height, so it never pierces the membrane.

    Volumes, exactly

    Every membrane is closed and oriented outward, so the divergence theorem turns the volume inside it into a sum over its triangles (a,b,c):

    V=\frac{1}{6}\sum_{\triangle}\, a\cdot(b\times c)

    For the sliders above, each polygonal frustum of the spine has the exact volume \tfrac{h}{3}\big(A_0+\sqrt{A_0A_1}+A_1\big), with A=\tfrac{n}{2}r^2\sin\tfrac{2\pi}{n}.

    Tab 02 · Process Spine

    A real spine, step by step.

    Spines reconstructed from electron microscopy (here one of the 4,223 Kasthuri et al. 2015 spines) arrive as rough, voxel-stepped surfaces in the microscope's coordinates. Six steps, one subtab each, turn one into a clean spine on a dendrite, in the frame of the ideal spine.

    1. Select and reorient

      Click the base of the spine. A plane is fitted through the picks, and the spine is turned so its normal points up (+y), the base at the origin.

    2. Process and cut

      Smooth away the voxel steps, coarsen to the edge length of the Vlachos spines, and cut the base open along a plane you can drag.

    3. Synapse

      A sphere follows the mouse; a click makes the patch of membrane inside it the postsynaptic density, split exactly along the sphere.

    4. Spine apparatus

      An organelle along the spine's centerline: a tube, a fitted tube, a cluster of spheres, or the spine itself, contracted.

    5. Tubes

      A dendrite under the spine and an ER inside it, welded to the spine and to its spine apparatus into closed membranes.

    6. Tetrahedralize

      Measurement zones, then a tetrahedral volume mesh by TetGen, with every membrane kept as a labeled surface.

    The math

    1 · Fitting the base

    The base plane through the picked points x_i minimizes the squared distances. Its center is their mean c, its normal the right singular vector of the smallest singular value:

    n=\arg\min_{\|n\|=1}\sum_i\big(n\cdot(x_i-c)\big)^2

    The smallest rotation turning n onto e_y, with v=n\times e_y and c=n\cdot e_y:

    R=I+[v]_\times+\frac{[v]_\times^{2}}{1+c}

    2 · Smoothing without shrinking

    Plain Laplacian smoothing shrinks a surface. Taubin's method follows each smoothing step with a slightly larger step back, which cancels the shrinkage but not the smoothing:

    x\leftarrow x+\lambda\,\Delta x,\qquad x\leftarrow x+\mu\,\Delta x,\qquad \lambda=0.5,\ \mu=-0.53

    with \Delta x the mean of a vertex's neighbors minus the vertex, repeated 20 times.

    2 · Coarsening

    Edges are collapsed until the target length (0.068 µm). Each vertex carries the Garland–Heckbert quadric of the planes p=(a,b,c,d) of its triangles, and the merged vertex goes where it is smallest:

    Q=\sum_{p} p\,p^{\mathsf T},\qquad \bar x=\arg\min_x \begin{pmatrix}x\\1\end{pmatrix}^{\!\mathsf T}\!Q\begin{pmatrix}x\\1\end{pmatrix}

    A collapse that would move the surface more than 0.03 µm is refused.

    2 · Opening the base

    The base is cut along a plane, the crossing triangles split exactly. The opening is accepted only if it is a single loop, star-shaped around its center o (every point of it visible from o), so it can be joined to a tube:

    \operatorname{sign}\Big(\big((p_{k}-o)\times(p_{k+1}-o)\big)\cdot e_y\Big)\ \text{the same for every edge } (p_k,p_{k+1})

    5 · Welding to the dendrite

    The opening is dropped onto the tube and surrounded by a graded patch of rings, from the opening's edge length h_0 out to the tube's, each ring wider than the last:

    h_k=\min\!\big(h_0\,g^{k},\,h_{\text{tube}}\big),\qquad g=1.3

    The crease where the two meet is rounded: vertices within a radius \rho move toward the mean of their neighbors by \tfrac12 w, with w=(1-d/\rho)^2.

    6 · Tetrahedralization

    The last step fills the closed membranes with tetrahedra (TetGen), keeping every membrane as a labeled surface and cutting the measurement zones along exact planes. It's the heaviest step and the one that needs the cleanest input, which is why steps 1 to 5 check the surfaces so carefully. It's mentioned here only; the meshes on this page are the surfaces.

    Tab 03 · Cell Builder

    A whole neuron, with spines.

    From a 1D reconstruction (an .swc file: points with radii, joined as a tree) to the membranes of the whole cell, made by AnaMorph, with the ER as a thinner copy inside. Then real spines are welded onto it, each with its own synapse and its real spine apparatus, joined to the ER. The background flies from the whole cell down to its spines as you scroll.

    Built with AnaMorph

    The cell's surfaces come from AnaMorph (NeuroBox3D), which builds watertight neuron surfaces from 1D morphologies. SpineBuilder runs its am_cellgen twice, once for the plasma membrane and once with every radius scaled down for the ER, then checks the result and welds in the spines and their spine apparatus itself.

    github.com/NeuroBox3D/AnaMorph →

    The math

    From a skeleton to a surface

    An .swc morphology is a tree of points x_i with radii r_i. AnaMorph fits splines \gamma(t) with radii r(t) through each branch and wraps them in canal surfaces, the envelope of the balls along the curve:

    S=\partial\bigcup_{t} B\big(\gamma(t),\,r(t)\big)

    then meshes the union of all the branches and the soma into a single surface.

    The ER inside

    The ER is the same morphology with every radius scaled by a ratio s, applied after AnaMorph's own preconditioning, so both membranes share one centerline:

    r^{er}_i=s\,r^{pm}_i,\qquad 0.1\le s<1

    Scaling the .swc beforehand instead gave over 100 times more membrane crossings.

    Checking the surfaces

    A closed, orientable surface of genus g has Euler characteristic

    \chi=V-E+F=2-2g

    Each membrane must have \chi=2, every edge in exactly two triangles, used once in each direction. A point is inside a membrane when a ray from it crosses the membrane an odd number of times, which checks that the ER lies inside the plasma membrane.

    Placing the spines

    Sites are drawn uniformly over the membrane: a triangle is picked with probability proportional to its area, then a point in it,

    P(T)=\frac{|T|}{\sum_{T'}|T'|}
    x=(1-\sqrt{u})\,a+\sqrt{u}(1-v)\,b+\sqrt{u}\,v\,c

    with u,v uniform in [0,1], away from the soma. At each site the membrane is refined by longest-edge bisection, a hole is cut along the neck, and a collar of triangles zips the neck in.

    The whole cell can be tetrahedralized too (about 900,000 tetrahedra for this cell with 10 spines). That step is left out here.

    Open it in ProMesh

    Every mesh is a .ugx file.

    SpineBuilder saves ug4's grid format, so its meshes open directly in ProMesh, the mesh editor of the ug4 project, where each subset (membrane, synapse, ER, spine apparatus, volumes) can be shown, hidden and cut.

    1. Open the .ugx

      File → Open, or give the file to ProMesh on the command line.

    2. Solid surfaces

      Hide the vertices and edges to see the membranes.

    3. The subsets

      Every subset in the Scene Inspector, each turned off and on.

    4. A clip plane

      Cut through the dendrite and the spine.

    5. Inside the cut

      The membranes hidden, the volume subsets in the cut.
