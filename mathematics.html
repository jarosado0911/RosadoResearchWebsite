Complex analysis · Fourier series · 2014–2020

Series, integrals and analysis.

At Rowan I worked with Dr. Thomas J. Osler on an unusual way to evaluate definite integrals: never integrate at all. Match the coefficients of two series of the same function, and the integrals fall out. That gave a table of 36 integrals, 25 of which we couldn't find anywhere. Below it are my other talks and papers in analysis, from Cauchy's theorem and the Riemann hypothesis to elliptic PDEs and image segmentation.

01 · The method

Two series, one function.

Instead of starting from an integral, start from a function whose Taylor series you know. Put it on a circle in the complex plane; its real and imaginary parts are then Fourier series in the angle. Fourier coefficients are integrals, and Taylor coefficients are known numbers. Setting them equal evaluates the integrals.

  1. 1

    A known Taylor series

    g(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_nz^n
  2. 2

    On the circle z=re^{i\theta}

    \begin{aligned}g(re^{i\theta})=\;&c_0+\sum_{n\ge1}c_nr^n\cos n\theta\\&+i\sum_{n\ge1}c_nr^n\sin n\theta\end{aligned}
  3. 3

    Split into real and imaginary parts

    g(re^{i\theta})=u(r,\theta)+i\,v(r,\theta)
  4. 4

    Read them as Fourier series in \theta, so

    \begin{gathered}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}u\,d\theta=2\pi c_0\\\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}u\cos n\theta\,d\theta=\pi c_nr^n\\\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}v\sin n\theta\,d\theta=\pi c_nr^n\end{gathered}

From the table

No. 14 · from e^z

\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{r\cos\theta}\cos(r\sin\theta)\cos(n\theta)\,d\theta=\frac{\pi r^n}{n!}

No. 17 · from \tan z, with Bernoulli numbers

\begin{aligned}&\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{\sin(2r\cos\theta)\cos((2n-1)\theta)}{\cos(2r\cos\theta)+\cosh(2r\sin\theta)}\,d\theta\\&\quad=\frac{(-1)^{n-1}2^{2n}(2^{2n}-1)B_{2n}\,\pi r^{2n-1}}{(2n)!}\end{aligned}

No. 26 · from e^z/(1-z)

\begin{aligned}&\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{e^{r\cos\theta}\big(\cos(r\sin\theta)(1-r\cos\theta)-r\sin\theta\sin(r\sin\theta)\big)}{1+r^2-2r\cos\theta}\cos(n\theta)\,d\theta\\&\quad=\pi r^n\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac1{k!}\end{aligned}

No. 35 · from \sqrt{z+1}

\begin{aligned}&\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\cos\!\Big(\tfrac12\arctan\tfrac{r\sin\theta}{1+r\cos\theta}\Big)\sqrt[4]{1+r^2+2r\cos\theta}\,\cos(n\theta)\,d\theta\\&\quad=\frac{(-1)^{n+1}(2n-3)!\,\pi r^n}{2^{2n-2}\,n!\,(n-2)!}\end{aligned}

Of the 36, seven were known (Nos. 1, 6, 13, 19, 25, 28, 31) and four are zero because the integrand is odd (Nos. 7, 10, 16, 22). Mathematica and the standard tables (Gradshteyn–Ryzhik, Brychkov) gave no closed form for the other 25; we checked those numerically.
