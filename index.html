Research

How neurons compute with calcium.

Multiscale models that keep the cell's real shape, from nanometer spines to whole neurons.

Calcium in dendritic spines

Ultrastructural simulations of spine-to-dendrite calcium signaling showed that the spine apparatus can act as an all-or-nothing switch between spine head and dendrite.

Explore the 3D spine gallery → Inside SpineBuilder, the mesh builder → The full calcium simulation: one synapse, a whole neuron →

Brain stimulation (TMS)

A multiscale toolbox that couples the electric fields of transcranial magnetic stimulation to neuron electrical and calcium activity.

Resilience & Alzheimer's disease

A model of how neurons keep calcium signaling working despite synapse loss and calcium leaks, with implications for Alzheimer's disease.

About

Where mathematics, neuroscience and software meet.

I started as an electrical and computer engineer, became a mathematics teacher, and went on to a Ph.D. in mathematics at Temple University. My dissertation modeled calcium dynamics in neurons under transcranial magnetic stimulation, down to the ultrastructure of single dendritic spines.

Since then I've worked as an applied research mathematician and software engineer on machine learning, high-performance computing and infrastructure automation. I still build neuron simulations, from finite element calcium models to the VR simulator behind this page's background.

I work in C/C++, Python, C#/Unity, MATLAB, Mathematica and Lua, on Linux HPC systems. I speak English and Spanish.
