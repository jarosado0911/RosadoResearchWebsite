Research · Dendritic spines · Ten spines Research mode

Ten spines, one calcium wave.

The next step after one synapse on a whole neuron: the same reconstructed cell, now with ten dendritic spines grown onto its dendrites. Each spine carries a spine apparatus, a thin finger of the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) that reaches up into the spine and stores calcium. All ten synapses open at the same moment. Calcium floods the spine heads, every spine apparatus empties within a few milliseconds, and the ER below them carries the signal on as a wave through the whole cell. The background is that simulation, replayed from its own output. Scroll to follow it; drag to turn the cell.

Research mode. This is work in progress, shown as it happens: one simulation run, with the parameters of my earlier spine models, not yet repeated on a finer mesh or with smaller time steps, and not yet published. The numbers on this page describe this run; they are not settled results.

01 · The wave

Follow the calcium.

Scroll through the run. The background flies to each part of the cell and plays that stretch of time, slowed down where the spines are; the player at the bottom right pauses it or scrubs through all 100 ms.

  1. 01

    A neuron with ten spines

    A rat pyramidal neuron, 0-2a from NeuroMorpho.org, with an ER tube inside every dendrite at half its radius. Ten real spine shapes were grown onto it with SpineBuilder, each with a spine apparatus whose lumen is one with the dendrite's ER. Five spines sit within 40 µm of the soma, four between 58 and 109 µm, and one, syn7, 344 µm out on the long apical dendrite.

    The membrane glows with the calcium in the cytosol just inside it. The ER is colored by the calcium it stores, on the same jet scale: dark red while full, at 250 µM, through yellow and green to blue as it empties.

  2. 02

    Ten synapses open at once

    At t = 0 every synapse opens together. For 10 ms they let calcium in, about 118,000 ions in all, and for 200 ms they release IP3, a second messenger that also opens the ER. Each synapse glows with the calcium measured right at it: peaks between 5.5 and 71 µM, set by the synapse's size and the shape of its spine.

  3. 03

    Inside a spine

    The membranes open along syn8's axis to show the calcium inside it: the cytosol of the spine head, and the lumen of the spine apparatus. Calcium pours in at the synapse; within a millisecond the spine apparatus answers, its receptors open and its store pours out. It turns from red to blue: half empty at 1.0 ms, settled near 25 µM.

    In every one of the ten spines the spine apparatus is half empty within 0.8 to 2.5 ms.

  4. 04

    A spine with two heads

    syn10 is branched: one neck, and a spine apparatus that splits in two, one branch for each head. It is the largest spine apparatus of the ten, and the slowest and least to empty: half empty only at 2.5 ms, and it settles at 51 µM where the others reach 25–46.

  5. 05

    Down into the dendrite

    Each spine apparatus is joined to the ER of the dendrite below it, and that ER releases next: within 10 µm of a synapse, half of it has released by 5.5 ms. From there the release spreads along the dendritic ER, red giving way to blue, at about 3 µm per millisecond.

  6. 06

    The soma empties

    The wave reaches the soma from the dendrites of the five nearby spines. Its large ER starts to release at about 10 ms, from the side facing those dendrites, then from its whole surface inward: half of it at 40 ms, nearly all by 50 ms. The released calcium stays close to the ER, a bright shell around it: on most of the soma's own membrane it stays near 0.05 µM.

  7. 07

    Out to the tips

    The front keeps going into dendrites that carry no synapse at all. Half of the cell's ER membrane has released by 26 ms, 90% by 50 ms, and 99.8% by 70 ms. The last to go are the far tips of the dendrites, 60–70 ms after the synapses opened.

  8. 08

    Two spines keep ringing

    Most spines settle after the pulse. syn3 does not: from 25 ms its synapse spikes again and again, eight times, about every 7 ms. It has one of the smallest synapses and a long, thin neck with a spine apparatus that runs almost its whole length. syn9 joins in at about 40 ms with smaller spikes. Why is one of the open questions.

  9. 09

    100 ms later

    Across the cell the ER is down from 250 to 55 µM on average, the cytosol sits at about half a micromolar, and the IP3 from the synapses is still rising. Refilling the stores takes far longer than this run.

03 · The stores empty

Spine first, then dendrite, then soma.

How much of each store has released, that is, fallen below half of its resting calcium, over the run. The spine apparatuses go first, nine tenths of them within 3 ms; the dendritic ER follows the wave for the next 60; the soma's large ER waits for the wave to arrive, then empties in a rush. Point at the chart to read it; click to send the 3D cell to that moment. The dashed line is its clock.

04 · What we're exploring

The questions this run opens.

My single-spine models showed that the spine apparatus can act as an all-or-nothing switch between a spine and its dendrite. This project asks what that switch does inside a whole neuron, with the cell's real shape, the ER in every branch and many spines at once.

Spines as triggers

Does the spine apparatus start the wave?

Here every spine apparatus empties first and the dendritic ER beneath it follows. Is the spine apparatus the spark that turns a local synaptic signal into a cell-wide wave, or would the dendrite's own ER have started it anyway? The same cell with spines that lack one would tell.

How many, and where

From ten spines to hundreds

Ten synapses opening together is a strong, perfectly timed stimulus. Meshes of the same cell with 20, 40, 80, 160 and 320 spines are already built, to ask how the wave's start and reach depend on how many spines are active, when, and where they sit.

Oscillations

Why do some spines ring?

syn3 and syn9 keep spiking long after their synapses closed, about every 7 ms. A cycle of release, refilling by the ER's pumps, and release again is the likely explanation, but it has not been analysed yet, nor checked on a finer mesh.

Calcium meets voltage

Driven by the cell's own spikes

In this run only the synapses let calcium in. The next run, on the supercomputer now, adds voltage-gated calcium channels across the whole membrane, opened by a simulated burst of action potentials. For now the coupling runs one way: voltage moves calcium, not back.

The thick dendrite

Why does the wave get through this time?

In the one-synapse run the wave stalled in the thick apical dendrite. Here the ER of the whole cell releases, that dendrite included. But the cell changed in several ways at once: a wider ER, ten synapses instead of one, spines with their own stores. Separating them is next.

Before any claim

Is it the mesh?

One run on one mesh. Before the numbers here become results, they need the same run on a finer mesh and with smaller time steps, and the oscillations and the wave speed have to survive both.

05 · The run

What is in the model.

The whole-cell version of my spine calcium model, in UG4 with the neuro_collection plugin, run on SDSC Expanse under my NSF ACCESS allocation for this project.

The cellRat pyramidal neuron 0-2a (NeuroMorpho.org); the ER as a tube at half the radius of every dendrite and a ball in the soma; ten spines with their spine apparatus placed and welded in by SpineBuilder
The mesh1,045,560 tetrahedra on 223,657 vertices: cytosol, ER and spine apparatus lumen, with the plasma membrane, ER membrane, spine apparatus membrane and ten synapses as their own surfaces
In the cytosolFree calcium, a calcium buffer (calbindin) and IP3, all diffusing
In the ERFree calcium, diffusing through the dendrites' ER into every spine apparatus and back
On the ER and spine apparatusRyanodine receptors and IP3 receptors that release calcium, SERCA pumps that take it back, a small leak; the same on the spine apparatus as on the dendritic ER
On the plasma membranePMCA and sodium-calcium exchanger pumps that move calcium out of the cell, a small leak in; at rest every flux balances
The stimulusAll ten synapses at t = 0: calcium for 10 ms, IP3 for 200 ms
The numericsFinite volumes in space, implicit time steps of 5 µs: 20,000 steps, Newton's method converging at every one
The hardware128 cores on one SDSC Expanse node, 6 h 39 min, 852 core-hours; 101 snapshots, one every millisecond, 5.7 GB
