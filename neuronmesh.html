From a skeleton to a closed surface.

A neuron reconstruction is a tree of points with radii. A simulation, or a VR view, needs a surface: one closed membrane, with no tubes cutting into each other. NeuronMesh is the C++ tool I wrote to make it, after a first attempt in Python taught me where that's hard. It is still in development. The background follows one real cell from NeuroMorpho.org through every step.

The pipeline

Seven steps, one cell.

The cell is 0-2a.CNG.swc, a rat layer-5 pyramidal neuron: 457 points. Scroll through the steps; the background shows each one on the real cell.

  1. 01

    The reconstruction

    An .swc file from NeuroMorpho.org: every point has a position, a radius, a type (soma, axon, dendrite) and a parent, so the points form a tree rooted at the soma.

    \mathcal T=\{(x_i,\,r_i,\,\text{type}_i,\,\text{parent}_i)\}_{i=1}^{457}

    Between branch points and tips the tree splits into trunks, unbranched runs of points. This cell has 30.

  2. 02

    A smooth centerline

    Each trunk's points P_i=(x_i,y_i,z_i,r_i) become the control polygon of a clamped B-spline in \mathbb R^4, so position and radius are smoothed together:

    C(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}N_{i,d}(t)\,P_i,\qquad t\in[0,1]

    The repeated end knots make the curve pass through both ends of the trunk, so neighbors still meet at their branch point. It is then resampled at equal arc length \delta, here 4 µm, with N=\max\big(4,\operatorname{round}(L/\delta)\big) points: the 1D skeleton.

  3. 03

    Frames that don't twist

    To place a ring around each point, every point needs two directions across the curve. Rotation-minimizing frames (red and green here) turn only as much as the curve does, never spinning about it. See the frames below.

  4. 04

    Rings

    Around point p_i, with radius \rho_i and frame (t_i,r_i,s_i), a ring of n=12 vertices:

    x_{i,j}=p_i+\rho_i\big(\cos\theta_j\,r_i+\sin\theta_j\,s_i\big),\qquad \theta_j=\tfrac{2\pi j}{n}
  5. 05

    Tubes, each on its own

    Consecutive rings are joined by quads, split into triangles. Done trunk by trunk, this is where my first version stopped: every branch is a separate tube, and at the branch points they cut into each other and leave gaps. The background shows that mesh, from PythonNeuronMeshes.

  6. 06

    Branch points

    NeuronMesh stops each tube about a radius short of the branch point, and keeps trimming while two tube ends still overlap, \lVert e_a-e_b\rVert<r_a+r_b. Facing half-rings are then joined by cubic Bézier rails, pulled toward the branch point O:

    \begin{gathered}\begin{aligned}B(t)&=(1-t)^3W_i+3(1-t)^2t\,U_i\\&\quad+3(1-t)t^2\,U_j+t^3W_j,\end{aligned}\\U_i=O+\alpha(W_i-O)\end{gathered}

    with \alpha=0.5. The two holes left at the poles are closed by fans, and tips by a fan from the last ring's center.

  7. 07

    Welding into the soma

    The soma is a sphere of center c and radius R. Each tube is cut exactly where it enters it: along each generator, segment p\to p+d meets the sphere at p+\tau d, with w=p-c and

    \begin{gathered}\tau=\frac{-b-\sqrt{b^2-4a\gamma}}{2a},\\a=d\cdot d,\quad b=2\,w\cdot d,\quad \gamma=w\cdot w-R^2\end{gathered}

    These points form the seam, the exact curve where tube and sphere meet. The sphere is refined around each seam by longest-edge bisection, a hole is cut, and a collar of rings with geometric radii \rho_{i}=r_{\text{in}}(r_{\text{out}}/r_{\text{in}})^{i/(m+1)} joins the seam to it.

  8. ✓

    One closed surface

    26,564 triangles, one piece, no open edges, every edge in exactly two triangles, Euler characteristic

    \chi=V-E+F=13{,}284-39{,}846+26{,}564=2

    as a sphere. Every vertex also records which 1D points it belongs to, for coloring the surface by a 1D simulation in Neuro-VISOR.

Frenet: follows the curvature

The Frenet frame takes the normal from the curvature, N=T'/\lVert T'\rVert:

T'=\kappa N,\qquad N'=-\kappa T+\tau B,\qquad B'=-\tau N

Where the curve straightens or changes the side it bends to (\kappa\to0), N is undefined and flips. A tube swept with it twists and pinches there.

Rotation-minimizing: no spin

A rotation-minimizing (Bishop) frame (T,r,s) has no component of rotation about the tangent:

T'=k_1r+k_2s,\qquad r'=-k_1T,\qquad s'=-k_2T

It is defined everywhere, also on straight stretches, and its normals are parallel transported along the curve.

2023 · the rotation method

PythonNeuronMeshes transported the normal by rotating it, at every point, by the angle between consecutive tangents about their common normal:

\begin{gathered}b_i=\frac{t_i\times t_{i+1}}{\lVert t_i\times t_{i+1}\rVert},\quad \varphi_i=\arccos(t_i\cdot t_{i+1}),\\v_{i+1}=R(b_i,\varphi_i)\,v_i\end{gathered}

Correct, but a trigonometric rotation per point, and each trunk started its own frame from scratch.

2026 · the double reflection method

NeuronMesh uses two reflections in planes per step (Wang, Jüttler, Zheng and Liu, 2008), fourth-order accurate and with no trigonometry: with v_1=p_{i+1}-p_i,

r_i^L=r_i-\tfrac{2\,v_1\cdot r_i}{v_1\cdot v_1}v_1,\quad t_i^L=t_i-\tfrac{2\,v_1\cdot t_i}{v_1\cdot v_1}v_1,\quad v_2=t_{i+1}-t_i^L
r_{i+1}=r_i^L-\tfrac{2\,v_2\cdot r_i^L}{v_2\cdot v_2}v_2,\qquad s_{i+1}=t_{i+1}\times r_{i+1}

Why the first version fails

It swept every trunk as its own tube, from branch point to branch point, and placed a separate sphere for the soma. Nothing joins them: the tubes of a branch point overlap each other, their end rings leave open slits beside the overlap, and every dendrite runs into the soma as an open tube. The result is 31 separate pieces, 30 tubes and a sphere, with 720 open edges.

What NeuronMesh does instead

It shortens the tubes at each branch point until they no longer overlap and fills the gap with a curved patch, cuts each soma tube exactly where it enters the sphere and welds it in, and closes every tip. The crossings it still has (100 pairs) sit in a few sharp branch points and in collars at the soma, a known open item.

Running it

One command each.

NeuronMesh

One closed surface of the cell, written as .ugx with its 1D skeleton:

neuronmesh -i 0-2a.CNG.swc --mesh --method BS --p 3 \
    --delta 4 --sides 12 --repair --sphere-weld \
    --junction-refine 0.5 --1d --ugx -o 0-2a.ugx

A Neuro-VISOR archive, five levels from 16 to 1 µm:

neuronmesh -i 0-2a.CNG.swc --vrn -n 5 --delta 16 \
    --method BS --p 3 --sides 12 --repair \
    --sphere-weld --junction-refine 0.5 -o 0-2a.vrn
--method BS --p 3
clamped cubic B-spline
--delta
point spacing along the skeleton, µm
--sides
vertices per ring
--repair
collapse the multi-point soma outline first
--sphere-weld
weld every branch into a soma sphere
--junction-refine 0.5
rings 0.5 × the radius apart near branch points

Against another generator

NeuronMesh and vr-grids.

Neuro-VISOR's meshes used to come from vr-grids, scripts that drive ug4's neuro_collection plugin. I built both archives from the same cell, matched their levels (1D spacing 4 to 0.125 µm), and compared them on every axis I could measure.

vr-gridsNeuronMesh
The whole archive380 s19.8 s (19× faster)
Surface at 4 µm7 pieces, 84 open edges1 closed piece

Twist over the whole cell2,300–3,800°30–40°

Where the twist comes from

vr-grids projects one fixed reference vector \mathbf r per neurite onto each ring's plane, \mathbf p=(\mathbf r-(\mathbf r\cdot\mathbf t)\mathbf t)/\sqrt{1-(\mathbf r\cdot\mathbf t)^2}. Its rate of rotation about the tangent is

\omega(s)=-\frac{(\mathbf r\cdot\mathbf t)\;\mathbf t'\cdot(\mathbf t\times\mathbf r)}{1-(\mathbf r\cdot\mathbf t)^2}

zero only when the dendrite bends in the plane of \mathbf t and \mathbf r, and unbounded as \mathbf t\to\pm\mathbf r. The rotation-minimizing frame has \omega\equiv0 by construction.

How it was measured

For every 1D edge, each vertex of one ring is paired with its neighbor on the next ring of smallest angular offset about the edge's axis; the segment's twist is the median offset over the ring. An untwisted sweep gives offsets near zero, a rolled frame the same offset on every vertex.
