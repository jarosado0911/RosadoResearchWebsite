Neuro-VISOR · Electrical dynamics

Touch a neuron, watch it fire.

Neuro-VISOR (Virtual Interactive Simulation of Reality) puts a reconstructed neuron in a virtual room and simulates it while you watch. Reach out and touch a dendrite: the VR hand clamps the membrane there at +50 mV, an action potential starts, and you see it sweep through the branches in real time. Connect two cells with a synapse and the spike crosses over.

01 · In VR

One touch, one spike through the whole cell.

Recorded in Neuro-VISOR itself (the synapseVisualization branch), not drawn afterwards: the simulator's own coloring of the membrane potential, captured frame by frame while the solver ran. A hand touches the tip of the dendrite farthest from the soma for 1 ms, on the cell's finest grid (refinement 7, 4,357 nodes). Left: the cell alone. Right: the whole cell in the VR room. One frame is 0.1 ms of simulated time.

  1. At rest

    Every node sits at −70 mV, its gates at their steady state for that voltage, so nothing moves.

  2. The touch

    The hand clamps one node at +50 mV. The clamp is a Dirichlet condition added to the already factored matrix, so it costs nothing to switch on.

  3. The spike travels

    Current flows to the neighbours, their sodium gates open, and the action potential regenerates itself node by node down the dendrite.

  4. Through the soma

    It reaches the soma 2.8 ms after the touch began and invades every other branch from there. Thin and thick dendrites conduct at different speeds.

  5. Recovery

    Potassium repolarizes the membrane; behind the spike the sodium gates stay inactivated for a few milliseconds, so it cannot turn back.

02 · The model

A Hodgkin–Huxley cable on the real shape.

The neuron is its 1D skeleton: a graph of nodes with a radius each, refined so no edge is too long. Along the arc length x the membrane potential V obeys the cable equation with Hodgkin–Huxley sodium, potassium and leak currents, in the Traub–Miles form used for cortical and hippocampal cells.

The cable

\begin{aligned}C_m\,\partial_tV&=\frac{1}{2R_a\,r}\,\partial_x\big(r^2\,\partial_xV\big)-\bar g_{K}\,n^4(V-E_K)\\&\quad-\bar g_{Na}\,m^3h\,(V-E_{Na})-\bar g_L(V-E_L)-I_{\mathrm{syn}}\end{aligned}

The radius r(x) sits inside the derivative: a thin branch has more membrane per volume to charge, so it conducts more slowly and loads its parent less. I_{\mathrm{syn}} is the synaptic current per area (section 03).

Gates

\frac{ds}{dt}=\alpha_s(V)(1-s)-\beta_s(V)\,s,\qquad s=n,m,h

Each gate relaxes toward \alpha_s/(\alpha_s+\beta_s). The cell starts at rest, with every gate at its steady state at -70 mV.

Traub–Miles rates [1/ms], v=V-V_T in mV

\begin{gathered}\alpha_n=\frac{0.032\,(15-v)}{e^{(15-v)/5}-1},\quad\beta_n=0.5\,e^{(10-v)/40}\\ \alpha_m=\frac{0.32\,(13-v)}{e^{(13-v)/4}-1},\quad\beta_m=\frac{0.28\,(v-40)}{e^{(v-40)/5}-1}\\ \alpha_h=0.128\,e^{(17-v)/18},\quad\beta_h=\frac{4}{1+e^{(40-v)/5}}\end{gathered}

With the threshold shift V_T=-55 mV. Where a denominator vanishes the code uses its limit.

03 · Synapses

Wire cells together with your hands.

In VR you place a presynaptic marker on one cell and a postsynaptic one on another (or the same) cell. When the voltage at the presynaptic node crosses +38 mV on its way up, the synapse activates at that time t_s, and the postsynaptic node gets a conductance-based current that decays from there. Three receptor models follow Rothman.

AMPA · fast excitation

\begin{gathered}I=g\,a(t)\,(V-E),\\ a(t)=\frac{\big(1-e^{-(t-t_s)/\tau_r}\big)^2\,e^{-(t-t_s)/\tau_d}}{a_{\mathrm{norm}}}\end{gathered}

g=25 nS, E=0, \tau_d=4 ms, \tau_r=0.2 ms. a_{\mathrm{norm}} scales the peak of a to 1.

NMDA · slow, voltage-gated

I=\frac{g\,e^{-(t-t_s)/\tau_d}}{1+e^{-(V-V_{1/2})/k}}\,(V-E)

The magnesium block: the channel only conducts once the cell is already depolarized. g=1 nS, \tau_d=50 ms, V_{1/2}=-12.8 mV, k=22.4 mV.

GABAA · inhibition

I=g\,e^{-(t-t_s)/\tau_d}\,(V-E_{\mathrm{GABA}})

E_{\mathrm{GABA}}=-80 mV, below rest, so it pulls the cell away from firing. g=1 nS, \tau_d=6.5 ms.

Stability

g\le 39\ \tfrac{\mathrm{nS}}{\mu\mathrm m^2}\sqrt{\tfrac{32\,\mu\mathrm s}{\Delta t}}\;A_{\mathrm{node}}

The synaptic current is taken explicitly, so a strong synapse on a tiny patch of membrane could blow up. Its conductance is capped by the membrane area of its node, a bound measured for the scheme.

04 · Numerics

Fast enough for VR, checked against NEURON.

A VR frame lasts 11 ms, so every step has to be cheap. The cable is linear and stiff, so it is taken implicitly; the gates and channel currents are nonlinear, so they are taken explicitly. The matrix then never changes and is factored once, when the cell loads.

SBDF2: second-order semi-implicit backward differences

\frac{3V^{k+1}-4V^k+V^{k-1}}{2\Delta t}=A\,V^{k+1}+2F(V^k,s^k)-F(V^{k-1},s^{k-1})

A is the discrete cable operator (a Hines matrix: tridiagonal along each branch, with extra entries at branch points) and F all ionic and synaptic currents. Every step solves \big(I-\tfrac23\Delta t\,A\big)V^{k+1}=r with the same sparse LU factorization (CSparse, minimum-degree ordering). The gates use the same two-level extrapolation. The first step, which has no V^{k-1}, is an IMEX Euler step with its own factorization, so the scheme stays second order from the start.

Touching without refactoring

x=y+Z\,(I-R\,Z)^{-1}R\,y

A hand or clamp pins a few nodes to a voltage. Instead of factoring a new matrix, the solver applies a rank-k (Sherman–Morrison–Woodbury) update to the existing LU: one extra solve per clamped node.

Validated

The solver was checked node for node against NEURON and the Rallpack benchmarks in a separate C# study. That study showed second-order convergence in time and fixed two conductances (\bar g_K, \bar g_{Na}) that had made spikes travel 2.6% too fast. The time step is capped at 32 µs, below where SBDF2 diverges on the test cells.
