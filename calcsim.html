Resilience & Alzheimer's disease · SIAM J. Appl. Math. 2023

Losing synapses, keeping the signal.

In Alzheimer's disease neurons lose synapses long before they die. We built a model that couples a neuron's voltage to its calcium (in the cytosol, in the endoplasmic reticulum, and bound to buffers) on the full branching shape of real reconstructed cells. Then we took synapses away to see when calcium signals still reach the cell body, where they control learning, plasticity and survival.

01 · In brief

Resilient to losing synapses, not to leaking ones.

Three changes that Alzheimer's disease is known to cause were put into the model one at a time: fewer synapses, calcium leaks at the synapses that are failing, and less of the calcium buffer calbindin.

Synapse loss

The neuron copes

Removing synapses step by step, the model neuron stays resilient: its calcium signaling keeps working with far fewer inputs.

Calcium leaks

The signal to the nucleus is lost

When the failing synapses also leak calcium (as amyloid-β pores do), neurons lose their ability to produce synapse-to-nucleus calcium signals.

Less calbindin

Partly recovered

With leaks present, downregulating calbindin partly recovers the signaling pathway to the nucleus, a possible lead toward stabilizing calcium signaling in the disease.

02 · Simulation

One synapse, one wave across the cell.

The model's default neuron, NMO_150605 from NeuroMorpho.org, with a single synapse at the tip of its longest dendrite. The synapse lets in a 10 ms pulse of calcium every 200 ms. Left: free calcium in the cytosol. Right: calcium inside the ER. Below each: the concentration at the four circled points.

Calcium in the cytosol (log scale) 0.05 µM40 µM
Calcium in the ER (log scale) 10 µM300 µM

  1. At rest

    The cytosol holds about 50 nM of free calcium (dark blue) and the ER is full at about 250 µM (red). Pumps and leaks balance exactly.

  2. The synapse opens

    At 20 ms calcium pours in at the tip (★) and reaches 27 µM there. Ryanodine receptors on the ER open in response and the ER at the tip empties.

  3. The wave travels

    Calcium released by the ER opens the receptors next to it: calcium-induced calcium release. The front passes the middle at 55 ms, the soma at 82 ms and the far tip at 103 ms.

  4. The soma fills

    The cell body fills slowly to 35 µM, at 157 ms, the signal a synapse sends to the nucleus. Behind the front the ER stays almost empty.

  5. Later pulses stay local

    The pulses at 220, 420, 620 and 820 ms light up only the dendrite near the synapse. The ER refills slowly, so there is little calcium left to release and no second wave crosses the cell. Near the end the far tip, whose ER has refilled, fires once on its own.

How it was made: this run uses the CalcSim calcium equations as I ported them to C# for Neuro-VISOR, my VR simulator, and checked them against an independent NEURON solution. It is not the paper's exact setup: it leaves out the voltage coupling (no calcium channels or Hodgkin–Huxley terms) and uses five times the paper's NCX density, the settings of the VR app. Mesh refined 16× (11,025 nodes), Δt = 6.25 µs, 1 s of simulated time in 5.5 minutes. Playback is 0.5 ms per frame up to 300 ms, then 2 ms per frame.

03 · The model

Two compartments, four species, one cable.

Each dendrite is a tube of radius r with a thinner ER tube of radius r_e=\tfrac38 r inside. Along the arc length x of the 1D graph, free calcium diffuses and binds to buffers in the cytosol (c, calbindin b) and in the ER (c_e, calreticulin b_e). The membranes exchange calcium through the fluxes J [mol m⁻² s⁻¹], scaled by the ratio of membrane area to volume.

Cytosol

\begin{aligned}\partial_t c&=D_c\,\partial_x^2c+k_b^-(b_{\mathrm{tot}}-b)-k_b^+\,b\,c\\&\quad+\frac{2r}{r^2-r_e^2}\,J_{\mathrm{PM}}+\frac{2r_e}{r^2-r_e^2}\,J_{\mathrm{ER}}\\ \partial_t b&=D_b\,\partial_x^2b+k_b^-(b_{\mathrm{tot}}-b)-k_b^+\,b\,c\end{aligned}

Free calbindin b binds calcium (b_{\mathrm{tot}}=40 µM). Binding takes both out of solution, so both equations get the same reaction term.

ER lumen

\begin{aligned}\partial_t c_e&=D_e\,\partial_x^2c_e+k_{be}^-(b_{e,\mathrm{tot}}-b_e)-k_{be}^+\,b_e\,c_e\\&\quad-\frac{2}{r_e}\,J_{\mathrm{ER}}+\frac{2}{r_e}\,J_{\mathrm{SOC}}\\ \partial_t b_e&=D_{be}\,\partial_x^2b_e+k_{be}^-(b_{e,\mathrm{tot}}-b_e)-k_{be}^+\,b_e\,c_e\end{aligned}

Calreticulin b_e buffers the ER's large store (3.6 mM total). Whatever the ER releases into the cytosol it loses, scaled by its own area to volume ratio 2/r_e.

Plasma membrane

J_{\mathrm{PM}}=-J_{\mathrm{PMCA}}-J_{\mathrm{NCX}}+J_{\mathrm{leak}}+J_{\mathrm{VDCC}}+J_{\mathrm{syn}}
\begin{gathered}J_{\mathrm{PMCA}}=\rho_PI_P\frac{c^2}{K_P^2+c^2},\\ J_{\mathrm{NCX}}=\rho_NI_N\frac{c}{K_N+c}\end{gathered}

Two pumps push calcium out: PMCA (high affinity, K_P=60 nM) and the NCX exchanger (high capacity, K_N=1.8 µM). A leak v_{lp}(c_o-c) lets it back in from outside (c_o=1 mM).

ER membrane

J_{\mathrm{ER}}=J_{\mathrm{RyR}}+v_{le}(c_e-c)-J_{\mathrm{SERCA}}
\begin{gathered}J_{\mathrm{SERCA}}=\rho_SI_S\frac{c}{(K_S+c)\,c_e},\\ J_{\mathrm{RyR}}=\rho_R\,(o_1+o_2)\,I_{\mathrm{ref}}\frac{c_e-c}{c_{e,\mathrm{ref}}}\end{gathered}

SERCA pumps calcium into the ER, ryanodine receptors release it when they are open (probability o_1+o_2). The two leak velocities v_{le},v_{lp} are chosen so that every flux balances at rest.

Ryanodine receptor kinetics

C_1\;\underset{k_a^-}{\overset{k_a^+c^4}{\rightleftharpoons}}\;O_1\;\underset{k_b^-}{\overset{k_b^+c^3}{\rightleftharpoons}}\;O_2,\qquad O_1\;\underset{k_c^-}{\overset{k_c^+}{\rightleftharpoons}}\;C_2
\begin{gathered}\frac{dc_1}{dt}=k_a^-o_1-k_a^+c^4c_1,\qquad\frac{do_2}{dt}=k_b^+c^3o_1-k_b^-o_2,\\ \frac{dc_2}{dt}=k_c^+o_1-k_c^-c_2,\qquad o_1=1-c_1-o_2-c_2\end{gathered}

Four states with cooperative calcium binding (fourth and third power). A little calcium opens the channel, and the calcium it releases opens its neighbours. That positive feedback, calcium-induced calcium release, is what carries the wave in the video.

Synapses

\begin{gathered}J_{\mathrm{syn}}(t)=a\Big(1-\frac{\tilde t-t_0}{t_1-t_0}\Big)\ \ \text{for }\tilde t\in[t_0,t_1],\\ \tilde t=t\bmod 200\ \mathrm{ms}\end{gathered}

Each synapse is a 10 ms pulse that ramps down from its amplitude a, five times a second. In the study, 1000 synapses get random places, start times (20–25 ms) and amplitudes.

Store-operated entry

J_{\mathrm{SOC}}=\rho_{\mathrm{SOC}}(c_e)\,\frac{I_{\mathrm{SOC}}}{zFA_o}\ln\frac{10\,c_o}{c_e}

When the ER drops below its resting level, Orai channels open (density \rho_{\mathrm{SOC}} grows with the depletion) and let it refill.

04 · Electro-calcium coupling

The voltage opens calcium channels, the calcium feeds back.

The paper's model is coupled both ways. A Hodgkin–Huxley cable carries the voltage along the same graph. Voltage-dependent calcium channels (VDCC) let calcium in, and above about 5 µM a calcium current adds to the voltage.

The cable

\begin{aligned}C_m\,\partial_tV&=\frac{1}{2R_a\,r}\,\partial_x\big(r^2\,\partial_xV\big)-g_Kn^4(V-E_K)\\&\quad-g_{Na}m^3h\,(V-E_{Na})-g_L(V-E_L)+I_{Ca}\end{aligned}

The gates follow \dot s=\alpha_s(V)(1-s)-\beta_s(V)s for s=n,m,h. The radius sits inside the derivative, so thin and thick dendrites conduct differently.

Calcium current

\begin{gathered}I_{Ca}=-g_{Ca}\,q\,\frac{K}{K+c}\,(V-E_{Ca})\,\sigma(c-5\,\mu\mathrm M),\\ E_{Ca}=12.5\ln\frac{c_o}{c}\ \mathrm{mV}\end{gathered}

A fast calcium conductance with gate q, inactivated by calcium itself, switched on smoothly (the sigmoid \sigma) once the cytosol passes 5 µM.

Voltage-dependent calcium channels

J_{\mathrm{VDCC}}=-\rho_V\,s_1s_2^2\,P_{Ca}\,\frac{zFV}{RT}\;\frac{c-c_o\,e^{-zFV/RT}}{1-e^{-zFV/RT}}

The Goldman–Hodgkin–Katz flux through N-type channels, with two gates s_1,s_2 relaxing to their voltage-dependent steady states. When the membrane depolarizes, the channels open and calcium flows down its 20,000-fold gradient.

05 · Numerics

Implicit where it's stiff, explicit where it's nonlinear.

Finite differences on the graph of the SWC file: a node's neighbours are its parent and children, so branch points need no special treatment. Diffusion and the cable are linear and stiff, so they are taken implicitly. The many nonlinear membrane fluxes are taken explicitly.

SBDF2: second-order semi-implicit BDF

\frac{3u^{n+1}-4u^n+u^{n-1}}{2\Delta t}=\mathcal D\,u^{n+1}+2F(u^n)-F(u^{n-1})

u=(c,c_e,b,b_e), \mathcal D the block-diagonal diffusion matrix and F all reactions and fluxes. The matrix I-\tfrac23\Delta t\,\mathcal D never changes, so it is factored once and every step costs one solve. The voltage gets the same scheme.

The graph Laplacian

(\partial_x^2u)_i\approx\frac{2}{\sum_{j\sim i}h_{ij}}\sum_{j\sim i}\frac{u_j-u_i}{h_{ij}}

Here h_{ij} is the length of the edge to neighbour j. On a straight run with equal edges this is the usual three-point stencil, and at a branch point it averages over all the branches.

The stiff little pieces

The RyR states are advanced with backward Euler, solved in closed form by the adjugate of a 3×3 matrix at every node. The VDCC gates take 25 forward-Euler substeps inside each step.

06 · The experiment

Take synapses away, then let them leak.

The protocol is in the repository, from the random synapses to the runs in parallel.

  1. Place 1000 synapses

    make_synapse_dist.m scatters them over the reconstructed cell with random start times and amplitudes.

  2. Remove them in steps

    Eighteen more synapse sets switch off 100, 200, … 900, then 910, … 990 synapses, leaving 10 active.

  3. Run every set

    Each set is a 4 s simulation (calcium_simulationSBDFfullcell.m), run in parallel with parfor, recording at every branch point.

  4. Add the leaks

    Switch on an amyloid-β pore leak k_\beta a^m at every inactivated synapse: the failing contacts now let calcium in.

  5. Lower calbindin

    Repeat with less calbindin (a lower b_{\mathrm{tot}}), which Alzheimer's disease is known to downregulate.
